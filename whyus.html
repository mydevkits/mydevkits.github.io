How we separate ourselves

Not a subscription.
Not an AI app builder.

There are two ways businesses usually get software: rent it forever, or lately, ask an AI platform to build it. We're a third thing, and the difference is who does the labor — and who pays for the mistakes.

No. 1

Versus subscriptions

A subscription is rent. The average small business pays about $4,500 a year for software it will never own — copy-print-and-stamp products stretched over millions of different customers, with 30–50% of the budget wasted on duplicate tools and licenses nobody uses. Miss a payment and the tool is gone, along with your data's happy home.

A MyDevKit ends the lease. One fee, one build, and the application runs on your own computers for as long as you want it to. No meter, no landlord, no renewal email every January.

No. 2

Versus AI app builders

"Isn't this just like those AI app builders?" Fair question — and no. On a prompt-to-app platform, you type what you want and the AI produces a rough draft. Then you pay — in tokens, credits, and hours — to fix it. Then fix it again. Build after build after build, each attempt on your dime, and the final draft often still isn't what you asked for, can't do everything you need, and lives on their servers under their monthly plan.

We already did that labor for you. Every MyDevKit is a product we designed, built, tested, fixed, and then actually ran — the Social Presence hub answers this website's chat right now. The kit you buy is the proven path with every wrong turn already removed: the AI still does the typing, but it follows a script that is known to end in a finished, working product.

That's why the guide is the whole product. You aren't paying for attempts. You're paying once, for the version that works.
