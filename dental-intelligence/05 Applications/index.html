Applications

Two tools, and what each one prevents

Both are working. Both sit before a failure rather than helping somebody recover from one, at the two points where an error is cheap to prevent and expensive to fix afterwards.

Built

The tools

Portability check

For sales · one field

Type a number, get an answer a salesperson can act on. No jargon. Under thirty seconds, because anything slower gets skipped under quota pressure by people who mean well.

Open it

Account record reader

For onboarding · 10 fields

Reads a carrier service record and extracts the fields a port order needs, with copy buttons, so nothing gets retyped and mistyped.

Deployment

How each one needs to be placed to work

Portability check
Inside the tool the reps already use, and as a required result on the opportunity before it can be marked closed won. An optional field is not a gate. It also needs a documented exception path with an answer inside one business day, because a gate with no way through it gets routed around rather than followed.
Record reader
In the hands of whoever collects account details before a port order is submitted. It removes transcription error as a category rather than reducing it, which matters because transcription is the one rejection cause that cannot be trained away.
Measuring them

How to tell whether they are working

Rejection reasons by category, month over month. Both tools target specific categories: the portability check targets numbers that were never movable, the record reader targets account number, name and address mismatches.

If those categories do not shrink, the tools are not working and I would say so rather than keeping them because they were built.
Still to build

What would come next

Open port order view
Every order in flight with its state, how long it has been there, and who is waiting on whom. A stuck port nobody is chasing is the most common quiet failure in this function, and it is invisible without a list.
Emergency address audit
A standing check that every live practice has a provisioned address on every handset, rather than a manual sweep. Given what it costs to be wrong, this should not depend on somebody remembering.
Rejection capture
Categorizing a rejection at the moment it happens rather than reconstructing it later. It is the number I most want to report and the one most easily lost.

All three depend on access to the platform and the ticketing system, so they are week four and later rather than now.
