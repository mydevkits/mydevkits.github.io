The three success measures

Where each one stands

SOPs and escalation workflow with DialStack, established and documented
Success measure one
Procedures written
  • 39 procedures covering access and accounts, shift operations, ticket handling and escalation, customer communication, 22 number and porting scenarios, phones and hardware, emergency calling, patient and card information, incidents, and the handoffs with other teams.
  • Each procedure has an owner, a version, a trigger, a definition of done and a failure path.
  • A document control procedure sets who may change a procedure and how.
  • The escalation standard is written. What leaves this team is the failing call, both numbers, the time with the time zone, what has been ruled out, and what is being asked for.
  • The workflow with DialStack I will establish in weeks eleven and twelve, since it needs a named contact and response expectations agreed with them.
Open the procedures
Both support specialists ramped and handling Level 1 tickets independently
Success measure two
Material and program ready
  • 14 training modules, each ending with what the person must be able to do before moving on.
  • 101 flash cards, 6 order of operations drills and 9 scenario tests alongside the reading.
  • Two weeks to independent on Level 1, which is the target, with the gate written in advance.
  • Porting takes until about week six, because a port runs ten to fifteen business days and a new starter cannot watch one complete faster than the carrier allows. That is a calendar constraint rather than a capability one.
  • The program starts on a new starter first day, so it is ready whenever the seats are filled.
  • I have run training across three offices in two countries and delivered company wide sessions to over a hundred people. The trainer will own this program rather than deliver mine.

Success measure three
Defined, instrumented from week four
  • Time to activation and health check completion, reporting to the Director of Onboarding.
  • Ticket volume, resolution time and satisfaction, reporting to the Director of Onboarding.
  • Underneath both, port success on first submission with rejection reasons by category.
  • Instrumented in week four, before there is enough volume to be interesting, since this function has no baseline and one cannot be reconstructed later.
  • Every speed measure is reported with a quality measure beside it. Resolution time goes with repeat contact rate.

Week one

What I need from you

These are the answers that unblock the most work. Most take a minute to give.

1
Accounts provisioned before my start dateDialStack, Bandwidth, the ticketing system, the customer relationship system and the product itself, each with the permission level confirmed rather than just the login. Salesforce matters beyond day one, because the portability check should sit in the tool sales already use.
2
One walkthrough of DialStack, screen by screenAn hour with whoever uses it most, recorded if possible. Every porting procedure names an exact step, and that hour turns those screen paths from described to specified.
3
The ticketing system, and reporting access to itThe ticket procedures are currently written without naming a system. Knowing which one it is sharpens them and unblocks the dashboard.
4
The DialStack contact and current escalation pathWhatever exists today, even if it is an email address. That is the starting point for agreeing response expectations.
5
How many practices are on the phone system todayAs distinct from the nine thousand on the platform. That number decides whether the first ninety days is a rollout or a pilot, and it changes the sequencing.
6
Whether the three roles are filledIf they are, week one is one to ones. If not, it is role briefs and interview criteria. Either is fine, and the plan is written to work both ways.
7
Any existing SOP template or documentation systemIf one exists, mine should conform to it rather than run alongside it.
8
The boundary with application supportSpecifically, when a practice reports that patient reminders are not going out, whose ticket is that. My working rule is to route on the symptom rather than the topic, and I would want to agree that with my counterpart rather than assume it.
Beyond the ninety days

Two changes I would like to look into

Separate from everything above, and not written as though they were mine to decide. Two places where the process asks a person to do work that repeats identically every time.

Letting a practice start their own migration
Idea, to be checked first
  • The practice uploads their account record, selects the numbers they want moved, chooses a date, and submits.
  • We make first contact already holding the record and already knowing which numbers can move.
  • Phone counts, emergency addresses and the website check still need a conversation, which is intended.
Letting a practice manage their own devices and emergency addresses
Idea, to be checked first
  • The practice sees their devices, changes an address, and submits it into the platform.
  • The flow then asks them to dial 933 and confirm what it read back, so it is not finished until it is verified.
  • This matters most for practices that move often, where a handset can be relocated without anyone telling us.

The first step on both is finding out whether the capability already exists. I am writing from outside and do not know what DialStack already offers or what is already planned. If either exists, that is a better answer than building anything.
