For the trainer

The training program, as a starting point

This is a proposed program, not a script. You own it. I have written it so there is something in place from day one rather than a blank page, and I expect you to change it once you have run it with a real person and seen where it does not hold.

How I would like to work

You own the method, I look at the outcome

If I specify how you train, you end up executing my program rather than building yours, and it stops improving the moment I stop paying attention. So the arrangement I would like is that the curriculum, the material, the delivery and the assessment are yours, and what we discuss is whether it is working.

The four things I would look at:

Time to independent
How long somebody takes to reach handling tickets on their own, and whether that is trending down as the program matures.
Residual questions
What newly ramped people still have to ask, and about what. Clusters point at a gap in the material rather than in the person.
Where mistakes cluster
Same principle. Three people making the same mistake is a training gap, not three individual failures.
Whether it changes
Material updated after an incident, without me asking. A program that has not changed in three months has stopped learning.
The question worth asking yourself regularly: what did the last new starter have to ask me that they should have been able to find? That answer is your backlog and it keeps the program honest.
The proposal

Two weeks to Level 1, then widening

Every week below is counted from that person own first day, not from mine and not from the department. The program is identical whether somebody starts next month or next year, because when these seats get filled is not something either of us controls. Gated on what somebody can do rather than time served, so it may run faster or slower for different people and neither is a problem. The gates are published in advance so nobody is assessed against criteria they have not seen, which I feel strongly about.

Their week 1
Foundations, then first tickets. The training manual and the drills. Sitting on real calls and reading real tickets, saying nothing. The output of this stage is questions, not knowledge.
Their week 2
Level 1 with support. They take Level 1 tickets from the queue. Somebody listens and debriefs after each one, and the prompting reduces day by day. The gate at the end of this week is independence on Level 1, which is the target the role sets.
Their weeks 3 to 6
Widening the scope. They are already carrying Level 1 on their own throughout this. What is still being added is porting, rejections and cutovers, and that takes four more weeks because a port runs ten to fifteen business days. The constraint is the carrier calendar rather than the person, and it is worth saying that to them plainly so the period does not read as being held back.

One request

Protect the widening period

Weeks three to six are what gets cut when the queue is deep, because by then they are already productive on Level 1 and it is tempting to leave them there. That produces somebody who is permanently a Level 1 specialist, which is not what was hired, and it leaves porting concentrated in too few hands.

If you are ever under pressure to compress the ramp, come to me rather than absorbing it. That is my problem to handle, not yours.
