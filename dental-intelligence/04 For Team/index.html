If you are new

Work through it in this order

Two weeks from your first day to handling Level 1 tickets on your own. Porting and cutovers take until about week six, because a port runs ten to fifteen business days and nobody can watch one finish faster than that. The stages are gated on what you can do rather than on time served.

  1. Read the training manual

    Fourteen modules, in the order you will need them. Start at the beginning and do not skip module seven on emergency calling. Open the training manual

  2. Drill what you read

    Flash cards for recall, order of operations drills for sequence, scenario tests for judgement. Reading alone does not stick. Open the practice tools

  3. Learn the actual steps

    Every job you will be asked to do, one step per screen, with the forms attached. Keep this open while you work rather than trying to memorize it. Open the walkthroughs

  4. Know how you will be assessed

    The gates between stages are written down in advance so nobody is guessing what the bar is. Open the program

Non negotiable

Six things that are never optional

Everything else in these documents is guidance you can apply judgement to. These six are not.

Emergency calling comes first, always On any contact where a practice cannot make calls, tell them in the first ten seconds to use mobiles for emergencies until we confirm the line is working. Then carry on. This comes before diagnosis, before apologies, before everything. Never test by dialing 911 and never ask a customer to. 933 is the test line and it reads back the address registered against that phone.
Every handset gets its own emergency address, verified with 933 Not one for the practice. And verified by dialing 933 from the handset and listening to what it reads back, not just by the platform showing it as submitted. An address that failed validation looks fine on our side and does nothing in a real emergency.
Nobody cancels anything until we confirm in writing A disconnected number cannot be forwarded. The account that owned it is gone. This is the most expensive mistake a customer can make and they make it with good intentions, to save a month of billing.
Never give a date the carrier has not given us A range that is explained holds up. A date that slips does not. Say what you are doing next and when you will next speak, which are both things you control.
Patient information never goes in a ticket Not in chat, not in email. Ask for what you actually need, which is almost always just the numbers and the time.
Update every thirty minutes during an outage Whether or not there is news. Still working on it, here is what we have ruled out, next update at half past, is a complete update. Silence is what turns a bad morning into a lost customer.
How we work

A few things about this team

Asking for help is not failing, but neither is working a problem. There is no minute count that means escalate. If you have genuinely been at something for about an hour and are not closing in, ask somebody to look with you. The exception is scale: a whole practice with no phones does not wait an hour.
Write your tickets as you go, not from memory at the end of the day. Detail decays within the hour, and the details that decay first are exactly the ones an escalation needs: the exact numbers, the exact time with the time zone, and what you already ruled out.
Two rejections in a row on the same port order is a process failure, not bad luck. Escalate rather than submitting a third time, and never resubmit the paperwork that just failed without getting fresh account details first.
If you follow a procedure and get a bad outcome, the procedure is wrong. That is a finding about the document, not about you. Raise it the same day, with the specific step, what you expected and what actually happened.
Everything here works offline in a browser and nothing needs installing. If something is missing, wrong or confusing, say so, and it gets corrected rather than worked around.
